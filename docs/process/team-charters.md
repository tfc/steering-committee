# Team Charters

> Reasoning, evidence, and objections: [the proposal](../proposals/decision-process.md).
> This document is rule text only. It sets out how the SC charters a team and what the person
> responsible for it may do.

---

## 1. Chartering decides two things

When the SC charters a team it decides **the remit** and **the responsible person**. Nothing
else. The SC does not select team members, and does not run recruitment.

## 2. What the responsible person does

- **decides, or delegates to the team, who joins and who leaves**, with no SC involvement
  either way;
- **sets the team's internal process**, which the team may run by consensus, by vote, or however
  it prefers;
- speaks for the team when escalating a question to the SC;
- answers to the SC for whether the team's work gets done.

## 3. The team organises itself; one person answers for it

1. **A team may organise internally however it likes**, including by majority vote. Its internal
   process is not the SC's business.
2. **Answerability does not transfer to the team.** *"The team decided"* is not an answer to the
   SC. The responsible person answers for every outcome inside the remit, including calls they
   argued against or were outvoted on.
3. The powers in §4 and the answerability in this section are one appointment. Neither is granted
   without the other.

*Why answerability cannot be shared: [the proposal][p-answerability].*

## 4. What the appointment grants, explicitly and in public

- **The power to decide inside the remit without asking.** No SC sign-off for ordinary work.
- **Protection from being overturned.** If a call inside the remit was wrong, the remedy is to
  change the remit or change the person (both public votes), not to reverse the call.
- **The right to say no.** To decline work, to set scope, to refuse a request, without being
  second-guessed for it.
- **The means to do the job**: access, tooling, and budget where the role needs them.
- **Public backing when a call is unpopular** and it was theirs to make.

## 5. The SC keeps two levers only

**Appoint** and **remove**. Both are public votes with reasons attached.

## 6. Grounds for removal

- the team's work is not getting done, and the responsible person has not said why;
- questions that belong inside the team keep arriving at the SC because the team could not settle
  them (a repeated pattern, not one genuinely hard case);
- the responsible person will not escalate when their team is truly stuck, leaving contributors
  with nowhere to go;
- the responsible person disowns a decision inside the remit rather than answering for it.

## 7. What is not grounds for removal

- **Disagreeing with the SC** is not grounds.
- **Making an unpopular call inside their own remit** is not grounds.
- **Being hard to work with** is the team's problem to solve, not the SC's.
